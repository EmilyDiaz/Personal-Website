X&D Advisor Hub

Problem

The X&D Advisor Hub has connected advisees and advisors for professional development since 2023 by using Microsoft Bookings. Through conversations with the two other program leads, I learned that the acquistion rate for onboarding had declined and that engagement for existing users had grown inconsistent because many advisee-advisor pairs only met once or twice before abandoning the program.

Solution

I built a gamified approach to the advisorship program by giving users a centralized dashboard where they could track their advisorship journey and relationships easily. Within a month after releasing, all 4 onboarders for the quarter registered in the app and 2 existing advisors rejoined the program.

Timeline

October 2025-March 2026

Role

0-1 Ownership. I researched, designed, and developed this project.

Research

I met up with two other leads in the program to discuss ideas on how to improve onboarding interest and keep retention high. They also both noted a lack of continous engagement, where advisees and advisors would fall out of the meeting cadence after the first interaction. The leads brought up different approaches they'd taken to make conversations during these meetings flow more naturally, such as offering pre-determined, session topics when booking meetings (such as Networking or Skills) but this still didn't seem to help much.

I began to research the gamification, what elements or standards it consisted of, and examples of gamification in the workplace. Inspiration for the app came mainly from video games and persona cards, the latter being an artifact used by UX designers on the early stages of design. Persona cards are used to identify key user types and humanize these user types, often giving them a name, personality, and likes and dislikes.

Choosing a Platform

Pros and Cons

Before presenting my idea to the other leads, I wanted to see what was feasible with the tools I had accessible to me on my company computer. I decided to go with the Microsoft Ecosystem for security, Entra ID integration, and customization. I then mapped the MVP user flow in Mural based on my decision and used it to align the other leads on scope before building.

Once shown, the MVP state was agreed upon by the leads, though they also required a list of advisees and advisors, editable goals, and a way to send feedback to the X&D Hub about the program and the app to be part of MVP.

Overcoming Obstacles

Growing pains with Power Platform

I'd initially wondered whether a single session was enough to count someone as an advisor or advisee, or whether it required an established cadence. Unfortunately, due to licensing issues, there was no way to connect Powerapps and Microsoft Bookings directly which prevented me from collecting session details.

I attempted to collect the information manually through automated emails sent for each sessions, but the formatting of Bookings emails in Microsoft Outlook put all information concerning who the meeting was booked with in HTML format, as part of the body of that message. When attempting to capture only the body of the email, Powerapps would cut out the part where it mentioned the advisor's or advisee's name.

Once the Bookings/Outlook limitation made session tracking impossible altogether, my original question became moot, manual assignment solved the immediate data problem, and users were left to define the relationship themselves.

The Gamified Dashboard

Although not as automated and a bit reductive, I introduced the solution of having users manually add advisors and advisees to their assignments page. This would give more control to the users allowing them to determine what they defined as an advisor or an advisee. Session count per advisor or advisee could not be collected anymore, but we could collect information such as job role, likes, dislikes, level, badges and avatar to still act as topic starters and spark a bit of a conversation. The design for the assignments page also stays true to the original inspiration: the persona card.

An onboarding process explaining how the dashboard worked/what to expect in the dashboard was provided only if it was the user's first time in the app. This stepped process would collect the information stated above and then neatly display it separate sections on the user's dashboard.

A fun and visually clean design system

Segoe UI was the selected font for the app, providing high readbility while maintaining a professional air for the app.

The main color used was a seafoam green with white as the backdrop. Green is often associated with success and learning, but a softer green was chosen as to create a more soothing environment, less focused on competition. It was also chosen to be less loud since it is a work environment but strong enough that it meets 508 color contrast standards against a white background.

The avatars were designed by me. I wanted a very diverse group of images for users to pick from, in a pixel art aethestic to keep the app fun and engaging.

Outcomes & Reflections

I transitioned off the project before seeing data past the first month. The in-app feedback button was used mostly to report bugs; a few users mentioned enjoying the app, though feedback stayed general rather than detailed. The clearest signal I have is adoption: within that first month, all four onboarders for the quarter registered, and two previously inactive advisors rejoined the program.

Choosing the Microsoft ecosystem meant accepting a real tradeoff: PowerFX and Power Automate have a learning curve, and I was the only one on the leads team building deep familiarity with them. I went in with some prior PowerApps experience and came out knowing far more, but that also meant the app's maintainability depended heavily on me specifically. To address that, I created a user guide for the other leads covering how to manage the PowerApp and the Power Automate flow, so the project wouldn't depend entirely on my continued involvement.

Having to balance my regular workload while researching, designing and building this app all by myself was quite a challenge, but it was also very exciting to see the app go beyond the Figma canvas and be utilized by the direct users.
