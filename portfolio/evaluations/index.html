Attorney Performance Evaluations

Problem

After adjudicating an Appeal, judges will submit an evaluation of the attorney's performance on the case. This evaluation is currently not available post-submission, which prevents judges and other authorized users from correcting or updating any feedback that might impact an attorney's pay and work opportunities.

Solution

Judges and other authorized users can now search for and edit evaluations after submission, as well as review the audit trail of changes. While one person edits, the case is locked to others, preventing duplication and overriding of information.

Timeline

March 2025-March 2026

Role & Team

Lead UX Designer

Team: 4 devs, 1 tech lead, 1 BA, and 1 QA

Research

Stakeholder interviews were conducted with 3 Veteran Law Judges and 2 Case Reviewers, the two types of users that would need access to an evaluation post submission.

Feature 1

Search Page

The original search page was outdated, seemingly abandoned as the rest of the application grew. A user could only search for Veteran IDs and their results returned only three types of cases: Appeals, Higher-Level Reviews and Supplemental Claims, where at this point the application had incorporated five types of cases, including performance evaluations. Load times were slow, given to the lack of table pagination and the large number of results returned.

Given that performance evaluations were a new case type that had to be integrated into the search page, I and the lead developer collaborated to design a solution that accommodated the new case type while improving the overall search experience. I redesigned the search page not only to include performance evaluations, but to improve the information architecture and modernize the tables so that load times were faster by including tabs and pagination.

Before

The idea of advanced search options was also discussed early on in the process of introducing performance evaluations into the search page. When it came to pieces of searchable information, what if a user did not have a Veteran ID, but the attorney's or judge's name, instead?

I designed for an advanced search drawer that gave users the ability to search for performance evaluations by several different criteria.

Feature 2

Case Details

A judge or case reviewer often has multiple cases assigned to them at a time and sometimes those cases are in the post-evaluation stage. I designed a quick way for users to not only access the performance evaluation from the case details page, but choose what version to access. I also designed for the locking mechanism to apply in this navigation menu as well, borrowing the idea from previous implementation in the Scheduling Hearings for Veterans case study.

Feature 3

Performance Evaluations Page

The new performance evaluation page was very similar to the pre-submission form. I had to design around an already dense form and add a change log for auditing purposes. I went with an accordion so the user only had to open it if necessary, in order to keep the form less dense and overwhelming on page load. Users also needed the ability to switch between historical and current versions of the performance evaluation, which was provided to them within the change log.

The previous form only allowed for one comment box, given that only one user was involved in the review and submitting process. Giving multiple users the ability to access performance evaluations post submission introduced issues with this sole comment box, such as comment overwriting. I also had to consider the introduction of versioning with the audit trail, and how comments would be associated with each version-both historical and current.

Before

Feedback and Iteration

After a few rounds of presentations, I got feedback that locking indication and preventation would also be needed at the search level. Stakeholders also wanted a quick way to see how many times a case had been edited for auditing purposes as well as an indicator for the editing expiration date of an evaluation.

Outcomes & Reflections

Unlike previous projects I had worked on, this project was handed off to three different development teams before it got properly worked on. The project was divided into two parts: Development team A would spearhead the case details entry point while development team B would take charge of the search page entry point. The entry point of the judges queue, although intially approved, was later deemed by stakeholders to be out of scope.

The initial development team did not have alot of experience working with functional stories, so I and the leads of the different development teams worked together on drafting business requirements. There were a few gaps missed during initial presentation of wireframes that had to be addressed during the UAT deployment in the development phase, but most of these gaps were linked to edge cases.

This feature was deployed to UAT right before I transitioned off the project, so I don't have visibility into production usage. Metrics I'd want to track include how often evaluations were edited post-submission and how much faster judges and case reviewers could resolve a correction through the application compared to the external workaround they used before.
